Conclusion
==========


This Technical Report addresses behavioural guardrails for agentic artificial intelligence in cyber-physical systems, separating behavioural safety from behavioural security while treating the cyber-physical system as the object of protection. It surveys the current landscape, practice and results, examines development trends, and conducts a technical analysis that identifies twelve attack surfaces, AS-01 to AS-12, across five classes. A risk taxonomy, RSK-01 to RSK-16, complements ten assessment factors, AF-01 to AF-10, together with their collection methods. The report then sets out a general assessment framework. It covers objectives, coverage, enforcement layers and trust boundaries, the rule of proximity, integration with the functional safety case, artefacts, roles, adoption, deterministic in-deadline fail-closed enforcement, placement, properties and minimum viable deployment. Standardization points follow, and the appendices record documented events, cases, instruments, technologies, related work and a mapping of existing instruments to those points.

On the technology side, the open work concerns what remains to be built and demonstrated. Instrumenting the observables is central, because behavioural guardrails depend on signals that can be measured at runtime. Deterministic in-deadline enforcement represents a further construction task, and the demonstration of fail-closed behaviour under fault conditions accompanies it. Measurement of drift and of guardrail effectiveness provides the evidence that such controls retain their function over time. The integration of guardrails with the physical safety argument completes this direction, because protection of the cyber-physical system depends on a coherent relationship between external controls and the existing safety case. This work is conceptual in character and points toward capabilities rather than particular implementations.

On the standardization side, the open work concerns what remains to be specified. A controlled vocabulary offers the foundation for consistent treatment across documents and implementations. A reference architecture and placement model set out where controls sit relative to trust boundaries and the physical boundary. A machine-readable guardrail policy and reporting format supports exchange and comparison. Identity and delegation requirements address the conditions under which an agent acts on behalf of another party. A testing and evaluation methodology provides a common basis for judging guardrail behaviour. A conformity route connects the technical controls to regulatory duties, so that assurance claims and oversight obligations relate to one another. These elements form a coherent programme for ITU-T and its partners.

The constructive conclusion of this Technical Report remains that safety is not sought by making the model more reliable, but by placing external, independent, mostly deterministic controls at every trust boundary the agent crosses. The two directions above, technology development and technical standardization, carry that work forward.
