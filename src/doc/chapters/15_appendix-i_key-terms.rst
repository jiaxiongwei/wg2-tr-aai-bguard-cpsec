Appendix I Key terms and descriptions
=====================================


For the purposes of this Technical Report, the following terms are described.

**agent identity:** A verifiable representation of an agentic AI system’s identity, including its origin, version, capabilities, and authority scope, used for authentication, authorisation, and accountability.

**agentic AI:** An AI system that exhibits goal-directed behaviour through autonomous planning, tool invocation, memory use, and action execution across multiple steps, potentially in collaboration with other agents.

**agentic behaviour:** The observable pattern of actions, decisions, and interactions that an agentic AI system produces in pursuit of its objectives, including planning sequences, tool calls, and responses to environmental feedback.

**alignment:** The degree to which the goals, values, and behavioural outputs of an agentic AI system correspond to the intentions and specifications of its deployers and affected stakeholders.

**behavioural assurance:** The set of evidence, arguments, and evaluation methods used to demonstrate that an agentic AI system’s behaviour satisfies its safety, security, and policy requirements throughout its operational lifecycle.

**behavioural risk:** The potential for an agentic AI system to cause harm through actions that fall outside its intended operational envelope, including goal drift, unintended tool use, or unsafe interaction patterns.

**behavioural safety:** The property of an agentic AI system whereby its observable behaviour remains within acceptable bounds of policy, human intent, and operational constraints throughout its deployment.

**content moderation:** The process of reviewing, filtering, or restricting AI system outputs that violate content policies, legal requirements, or safety standards, applied at either the model or application layer.

cyber-physical system: a working description used in this Technical Report for readability. It refers to systems that integrate computation, networking and physical processes, in which software components monitor or control physical entities and where cyber and physical effects interact.

**delegation:** The act by which a principal, whether human or another agent, transfers a defined task or decision authority to an agentic AI system, with associated scope and constraints.

**drift measurement:** The systematic comparison of an agentic AI system’s observed behavioural operator against its declared policy operator, performed over time to detect divergence from intended behaviour.

**goal drift:** The phenomenon in which an agentic AI system progressively diverges from its declared objectives during extended operation, often as a result of accumulated context, memory updates, or multi-step decision chains.

**guardrail:** A technical or procedural control that constrains, monitors, or intervenes in the behaviour of an agentic AI system to prevent or mitigate actions that deviate from intended policy or safety requirements.

**human oversight:** The mechanisms and processes through which humans monitor, interpret, and intervene in the behaviour of an agentic AI system, including human-in-the-loop, human-on-the-loop, and human-out-of-the-loop configurations.

**least agency:** A design and deployment principle whereby an agentic AI system is granted only the minimum level of autonomy, tool access, and decision authority necessary to perform its assigned function.

**memory poisoning:** The deliberate or inadvertent corruption of an agentic AI system’s persistent memory or context store such that subsequent decisions are influenced by malicious, biased, or incorrect information.

**red-teaming:** A structured adversarial testing process in which security professionals probe an AI system for vulnerabilities, misuse paths, and failure modes using techniques including prompt injection, tool misuse, and multi-turn attack chains.

**tool misuse:** The invocation of an external tool or API by an agentic AI system in a manner that exceeds its granted privileges, violates policy constraints, or produces unintended side effects.

The terms above are working descriptions provided for readability. Where a term is defined in a standard or other authoritative document, clause 3.1 governs.
